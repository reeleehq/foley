foley.level#

Level a clip to a stated loudness target for its class, so relative levels are sane.

A generated clip comes out at whatever level its model likes: Stable Audio 3 peaks near -20 dBFS, ElevenLabs much hotter, so two sounds meant to sit side by side differ by 10 dB or more before anyone touches a fader. level_bytes() measures a clip’s integrated loudness (ITU-R BS.1770-4) and scales it to the target for its class, then keeps the sample peak under foley’s ceiling.

The class targets are not new numbers. They are the dialogue-relative layer gains the WEAVE mixer already uses (foley.weave.mix.LAYER_GAIN_DB: a one-shot sits 9 dB under the voice, an ambience bed 21 dB under) applied to a voice at the reference programme loudness (foley.audio.DEFAULT_LOUDNESS_TARGET_LUFS, -16 LUFS). So a levelled one-shot is at -25 LUFS and a levelled bed at -37 LUFS: what they would be under a voice at -16 LUFS, and how they should sound side by side.

Two cases are left unchanged and said so, never amplified: a clip too short to measure (under one 400 ms gating block: a click, a page flick) and a near-silent one.

foley.generate() levels a clip when asked (level='sfx_fg', a Layer, or a LUFS number), for export and listening. It does not by default: the library keeps clips as generated, and the WEAVE render brings every clip to the reference loudness itself (below), so a stored clip’s level does not matter to the mix.

The WEAVE render levels by measurement (reference_gain_db(), used by foley.weave.render.FullRender): each clip is first brought to the reference loudness, then the layer gain is applied, so a hot ElevenLabs clip and a quiet Stable Audio one land at the same place in the mix and generate(level=...) stays an export tool. A clip too short for a loudness reading is levelled by its peak instead.

Zero-dependency import: numpy, pyloudnorm and soundfile load inside the functions.

foley.level.MAX_REFERENCE_BOOST_DB = 36.0#

The most a clip is boosted to reach the reference (dB), so a near-silent clip’s noise floor is not raised without bound. The 29 dB spread seen on a real film fits inside.

foley.level.REFERENCE_PEAK_CEILING_DBFS = -1.0#

Sample-peak ceiling (dBFS) the levelled clip must stay under. The render passes this after the layer gain (see foley.weave.render.FullRender), so a loud transient is not held back by headroom the layer gain is about to take anyway.

foley.level.SHORT_CLIP_PEAK_DBFS = -6.0#

Where a clip too short for a loudness reading (a click, a page flick) puts its sample peak. Provisional: the peak a typical one-shot has when its integrated loudness is the reference (about 10 dB of crest above -16 LUFS).

foley.level.reference_gain_db(clip, sample_rate, *, reference_lufs=None, peak_ceiling_dbfs=-1.0, short_peak_dbfs=-6.0, max_boost_db=36.0)[source]#

The gain (dB) that brings clip to the reference loudness; 0.0 if it cannot.

Integrated loudness (BS.1770, gated) is measured and the gain is reference_lufs - measured. A clip too short to measure is levelled by its sample peak (to short_peak_dbfs) instead; one that is long enough but below the loudness gate is near-silent and left alone. Either way the gain is capped so the clip’s sample peak stays at or under peak_ceiling_dbfs and the boost at max_boost_db; a silent or non-finite clip gets 0.0. Pure and deterministic.

Parameters:

  • clip – Mono or multichannel samples, time on axis 0.

  • sample_rate (int) – Sample rate in Hz.

  • reference_lufs (Optional[float]) – The loudness every clip is brought to (default: foley’s reference programme loudness, foley.audio.DEFAULT_LOUDNESS_TARGET_LUFS).

Return type:

float
