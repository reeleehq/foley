foley.weave.reaction#

Audience-reaction timing — a crowd answers an event, it does not coincide with it.

The mistake this module exists to prevent: syncing a cheer, laugh or applause clip to the instant of the on-screen event. A real audience needs time to see, recognise and react, so the reaction must start after the event has become legible, then swell, then decay. Placing it on the event reads as a laugh track pasted on the cut; placing it after reads as a room reacting.

Pure and stdlib-only. The numbers are working craft defaults (see REACTIONS), not measurements: published latency data for audience laughter/applause is thin, and the figures here are the conventional sound-editing ones (a beat of recognition, a swell of a second or so, a tail that thins out). Tune them per scene with latency=.

Rules encoded here (each is one keyword or field):

  • Legibility, not occurrence. legible_at is when the audience can see the event (an entrance when the figure is mostly on screen, a reveal when the new face has settled), and the latency is added to that, never to the start of the motion.

  • Rise, then fall. A reaction has an attack (rise_s) to peak and a decay_s tail; gain_points returns the envelope as (t, gain) breakpoints.

  • Second waves are smaller. A repeat of the same reaction peaks lower (REPEAT_PEAK), so cheers “die down” and bump again rather than climb forever.

  • Cut a crowd with a hush. hush_plan() places a “shh” after the applause has run its course and ducks the applause under it, so the characters can sleep.

foley.weave.reaction.hush_plan(applause, *, after=None)[source]#

Where a “shh” goes to quiet applause, and the applause cue ducked to match.

Returns (shh_onset, ducked_applause). The hush lands HUSH_AFTER_S after the applause peak (or at after), and the applause fades out over HUSH_DUCK_S from then, so the crowd is answering the hush rather than being cut.

>>> t, a = hush_plan(reaction_cue("applause", 8.0))
>>> a.end > t
True
